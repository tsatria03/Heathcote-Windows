Heathcote for Windows

Heathcote is an interactive audio adventure told through sound, first made by Fabulae Obscurae for the iPhone and iPad.
You follow a presenter exploring an abandoned asylum, Heathcote, while he is on the phone to his producer, and you choose what he does next.
The original was played by swiping on the screen.
This is a port of it for Windows, played with the keyboard and your screen reader.

What it has

The whole game can be played, from its opening and main menu, through the story, to its end credits, with the original's voices, music and ambience.
Your progress is saved as you play, so you can quit at any time and carry on later.
Every recording ships with the game, so nothing needs downloading.

Installing

Extract the whole Heathcote-Windows folder from the zip, and keep everything in it together.
Start the game with Heathcote.exe. Nothing else needs installing.
The story's recordings are in story.dat, in the data folder beside Heathcote.exe. The game cannot play without it, and says so if it is missing.

Headphones

Headphones are strongly recommended.
The original says so itself: "Heathcote is a responsive story told through sound. Headphones are strongly recommended."

Speech

The game speaks through NVDA if it is running.
Otherwise it speaks through another screen reader, such as JAWS or Narrator, and with no screen reader running, through a Windows voice.
It notices a screen reader started or closed while it runs.
Moving in a menu cuts off what was being said, so you never wait for a line to finish.
The window also shows what is said, for anyone helping you who can see the screen.

Starting the game

The game opens as the original did, with its police radio and the word Heathcote; Enter skips it.
The first time you start the game, it gives the original's advice: wear headphones, and try playing with your eyes closed. Enter continues, and the advice is not given again.
Then the main menu opens, with the original's title music playing under it:
Start story plays the story from its beginning.
Settings opens the settings.
Credits reads the original's credits, one at a time, with Back first.
Quit closes the game, as do Escape and closing the window.
Once a story is under way, Start story becomes Continue story, said with the story's chapter, such as "Continue story: approaching Heathcote.". It takes you back to the start of the part you were in, with its music and ambience as they were, whether you left by Return to menu or by quitting the game.

In every menu, Up and Down move, as do Left and Right, Home and End go to the first and last row, Enter chooses, and Escape goes back.
Moving past either end of a menu wraps round to the other.
Back is always the first row of a menu that has one, and the menu opens on it.
Rows are read without their place in the menu.

Playing the story

The story plays by itself, one part after another.
When a part ends and the story waits for you, you hear a short sound, and the same reminder every 7 seconds until you choose.
Up or Enter opens the actions menu, where you choose what the presenter does next.
In the actions menu, Up and Down move through the choices, as do Left and Right, and Home and End go to the first and last.
Each choice is played in the original's own recorded voice, and the choice plays again after 7 seconds if you have not moved.
Once a choice's recording has played, your screen reader speaks its words, the first time you land on it; moving on before the recording ends skips them.
Enter chooses, and the story carries on with your choice.
Escape closes the menu without choosing, and the story carries on where it was.
Some parts of the story must be heard before you can choose; while one plays, opening the menu is refused with a sound, until the story lets you.
A part you have heard to its end before can always be interrupted.

While the story plays:
Left rewinds a few seconds, and Right fast-forwards for a moment, with the voices kept at their normal pitch.
Escape pauses: the voice stops, the background sounds play on muffled, and the pause menu opens.
Tab says where you are: whether the story is playing, waiting at the end of a scene, or in the actions menu, with the chapter you are in, such as "Story is playing: working late.".
Switching to another window pauses the story, and coming back to the game carries it on by itself; a pause you made with Escape stays until you resume.

The pause menu:
Resume carries the story on, as Escape does.
Restart story asks first, with No first; Yes plays the story again from its beginning, and your progress in it is lost.
Settings opens the settings.
Return to menu goes back to the main menu with the title music; your progress is saved, so nothing is lost, and Continue story picks the story up again from the start of the part you were in.

The end:
When the story ends, the credits open while its last moments play on, with the ending music.
Back or Escape leaves them, and the main menu offers a fresh story.

Settings

Settings opens from the main menu or the pause menu, with these rows:
Back.
Master volume, Background volume, Interface volume and Title volume, each said with its level. Enter opens a list of its levels, from 100 percent down to 0, with Back first; Enter on a level sets it at once, and Escape keeps it as it was. Every volume can be set here, wherever you are.
Volume speed, how quickly a volume key repeats while you hold it, from 50 milliseconds, the fastest, to 500, the slowest, in a list of its own.
Key bindings.
Your settings are saved as you change them.

Volumes

The game has four volumes.
Each press of a volume key moves a volume by 10 percent and says where it is now, and holding the key keeps moving it, as quickly as Volume speed in Settings says.
Shift with Home and Shift with End turn the master volume up and down, from anywhere in the game.
The master volume sets how loud everything is together.
Page Up and Page Down turn the title volume up and down, wherever the title music plays: the main menu, and Settings and Credits opened from it.
During the story, and in the end credits, Page Up and Page Down turn the background volume up and down instead: the music, the ambience and the sounds that loop while you choose.
During the story, Shift with Page Up and Shift with Page Down turn the interface volume up and down: the game's own sounds. The recorded choices follow only the master volume, as the story's voices do.
The game remembers every volume.
Every step sounds like the same change, and 0 percent is silent.
In the pause menu, only the master volume's keys work, but Settings there can set any volume.

Key bindings

Press F1 at any time, or choose Key bindings in Settings, to change the game's keys.
Every action can be rebound, the menu keys included.
The screen opens on Back, and the actions follow it, each read with its keys.
The actions' names say where they work: Menus, Story, or Title music, and the master volume works everywhere.
Up and Down move through them, as do Left and Right, and Home and End go to the first and last.
Enter rebinds the action you are on: press the key you want, with Shift, Control or Alt if you like.
A adds another key for it instead of replacing the one it has.
Delete unbinds it.
R, then R again, resets every binding to the default; any other key after the first R keeps your bindings.
Escape, F1, or Enter on Back goes back.
The same key can do one thing in a menu and another during the story, such as Up and Escape.
If you give an action a key another action needs in the same place, the game takes it from that action and tells you which.
F1 and Escape can never be rebound, and the key bindings screen always uses its own keys, so you can always put your keys right.

Your files

Your settings, key bindings and story progress are kept in the Heathcote folder in your AppData folder, never in the game's own folder, so an update keeps them.
Every run writes a log there, in the logs folder.
If an error stops the game, it says so aloud and writes what happened to crash.txt in that logs folder.
